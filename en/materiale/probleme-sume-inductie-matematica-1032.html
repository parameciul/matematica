Practice problems: sums and mathematical induction

Published 26 October 2026 · by Laura Miron

Open PDF The PDF is in Romanian.

Summary

The problems are for students who want more practice with computing sums and with mathematical induction. They ask for writing sums with Σ, computing them with the properties and the standard sums, telescoping sums and proofs by induction, ordered on three levels: basic, consolidated and advanced.

Contents

  1. Basic level
  2. Consolidated level
  3. Advanced level

Press “Check” next to an exercise. Your answers stay on this device.

How to work: choose the level you start from. If you solve the problems of a level without trouble, move on to the next one. First reread the theory in the worksheet: the properties (P1)–(P3) and the standard sums (S1)–(S4).

Remember

$\sum_{k=1}^{n}{c}=n\cdot c$ $\sum_{k=1}^{n}{k}=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$ $\sum_{k=1}^{n}{k^2}=\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$ $\sum_{k=1}^{n}{k^3}=\left(\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\right)^2$

Induction: Step I $P(n_0)$ is true; Step II $P(k)\Rightarrow P(k+1)$; conclusion: $P(n)$ is true for every $n\ge n_0$.

Basic level

  1. Write out, then calculate:

    a) $\sum_{k=1}^{6}{(3k-1)}$;

    b) $\sum_{k=0}^{4}{2^k}$;

    c) $\sum_{k=3}^{7}{(k^2-1)}$;

    d) $\sum_{k=1}^{100}{\left(-1\right)^k}$.

  2. Write with the Σ symbol, after you find the general term:

    a) $5+10+15+\ldots+200$;

    b) $1+8+27+\ldots+1000$;

    c) $2+6+10+\ldots+(4n-2)$;

    d) $1\cdot 3+2\cdot 5+3\cdot 7+\ldots+n(2n+1)$;

    e) $\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{9}+\frac{1}{27}+\ldots+\frac{1}{3^n}$.

  3. Calculate, using the standard sums:

    a) $1+2+\ldots+200$;

    b) $1^2+2^2+\ldots+20^2$;

    c) $1^3+2^3+\ldots+10^3$;

    d) $2+4+6+\ldots+100$.

  4. Calculate, using the properties of sums:

    a) $\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(6k+1)}$;

    b) $\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(2k-3)}$;

    c) $\sum_{k=1}^{10}{(k^2+2k)}$.

  5. Prove by mathematical induction that, for every $n\in \N^*$:

    a) $2+4+6+\ldots+2n=n(n+1)$;

    b) $1+4+7+\ldots+(3n-2)=\frac{n(3n-1)}{2}$.

Consolidated level

  1. Write with Σ, then calculate:

    a) $3+5+7+\ldots+(2n+1)$;

    b) $2^2+4^2+6^2+\ldots+(2n)^2$;

    c) $1\cdot 5+2\cdot 6+3\cdot 7+\ldots+n(n+4)$.

  2. Let $S_n=1\cdot 2+3\cdot 4+5\cdot 6+\ldots+(2n-1)\cdot 2n$.

    a) Write $S_n$ with Σ and calculate it.

    b) Prove by induction the formula you found in a).

  3. Calculate:

    a) $\sum_{k=11}^{30}{k}$;

    b) $\sum_{k=5}^{10}{k^3}$;

    c) $\sum_{k=n+1}^{2n}{k^2}$.

  4. Find the nonzero natural number $n$ for which:

    a) $1+2+\ldots+n=78$;

    b) $1+3+5+\ldots+(2n-1)=169$;

    c) $1^2+2^2+\ldots+n^2=204$.

  5. Prove by mathematical induction that, for every $n\in \N^*$:

    a) $1^2+3^2+5^2+\ldots+(2n-1)^2=\frac{n(2n-1)(2n+1)}{3}$;

    b) $1+2+2^2+\ldots+2^{n-1}=2^n-1$.

Advanced level

  1. A telescoping sum.

    a) Check the equality $\frac{1}{(2k-1)(2k+1)}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot \left(\frac{1}{2k-1}-\frac{1}{2k+1}\right)$.

    b) Calculate $\frac{1}{1\cdot 3}+\frac{1}{3\cdot 5}+\ldots+\frac{1}{(2n-1)(2n+1)}$.

    c) Prove the result of b) by induction.

  2. Calculate $\sum_{k=1}^{n}{k(n-k)}$, for $n\ge 2$. Watch out: in this sum, $n$ does not depend on $k$. Check the result for $n=3$.

  3. Prove by induction that $\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2}{2^2}+\frac{3}{2^3}+\ldots+\frac{n}{2^n}=2-\frac{n+2}{2^n}$, for every $n\in \N^*$.

  4. Find the mistake. A student calculates like this: “$\sum_{k=1}^{n}{k(k+1)}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{k}\cdot \sum_{k=1}^{n}{(k+1)}$”.

    a) Check the equality for $n=2$.

    b) Which property of sums did the student apply wrongly?

    c) Calculate the sum correctly.

  5. Challenge. Calculate $1^2-2^2+3^2-4^2+5^2-6^2$ and $1^2-2^2+3^2-4^2+5^2-6^2+7^2$. What do you notice? Prove by induction that, for every $n\in \N^*$:

    $$\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left(-1\right)^{k+1}\cdot k^2}=\left(-1\right)^{n+1}\cdot \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$$

Frequently asked questions

How do I compute a sum whose general term is a product?

Expand the product into a sum of powers of the index, for example k(k + 2) = k² + 2k. Then split the sum into separate sums, take the constants out in front and apply the standard sums. Finally, factor the result and check it for n = 1 and n = 2.

What is a telescoping sum?

It is a sum in which each term can be written as the difference of two consecutive terms of another sequence, for example 1/(k(k + 1)) = 1/k − 1/(k + 1). When you write the sum out, the middle terms cancel in pairs and only the first and the last remain.
