Summary

A tautology is a formula that is true on every row of its truth table, and two formulas are equivalent when their columns agree. A compound statement is negated with De Morgan's laws and the negation of an implication. The worksheet trains truth tables, negating statements and the converse, inverse and contrapositive.

Remember

$A \equiv B$ means that the formulas $A$ and $B$ have the same truth values on every row of the table. A formula that is true on all rows is a tautology.

Frequently asked questions

What is a tautology?

A formula that takes the value $1$ on every row of its truth table, whatever the values of the variables. Simple examples are $p \vee \overline{p}$ and $p \rightarrow p$. To show that a formula is not a tautology, it is enough to find one row where it takes the value $0$.

How do I negate a statement that contains “and” or “or”?

You negate each simple proposition and switch the operator: “and” turns into “or”, and “or” turns into “and” (De Morgan's laws). For example, the negation of “$x > 0$ and $y > 0$” is “$x \leq 0$ or $y \leq 0$”. Relations are negated too: $<$ becomes $\geq$, and $\in$ becomes $\notin$.

Is the inverse of an implication the same as its negation?

No. The inverse of $p \rightarrow q$ is still an implication, $\overline{p} \rightarrow \overline{q}$, while the negation is $p \land \overline{q}$. The inverse always has the same truth value as the converse, not as the original implication.
