By the end of this worksheet you will be able to:

  • study the monotonicity of a sequence with the difference $a_{n+1}-a_n$;
  • study the monotonicity of a sequence with positive terms with the ratio $\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}$;
  • use induction to study the monotonicity of a sequence given by a recurrence relation;
  • choose the right method and show that a sequence is not monotonic.

1. Monotonic sequences

Definition

The sequence $(a_n)_{n\ge 1}$ is:

  • strictly increasing, if $a_n<a_{n+1}$ for every $n\ge 1$;
  • increasing, if $a_n\le a_{n+1}$ for every $n\ge 1$;
  • strictly decreasing, if $a_n>a_{n+1}$ for every $n\ge 1$;
  • decreasing, if $a_n\ge a_{n+1}$ for every $n\ge 1$.

An increasing or decreasing sequence is called monotonic.

A sequence is not monotonic if there are three consecutive terms that "go up and then down" or "go down and then up", for example $a_1<a_2$ and $a_2>a_3$.

2. The three methods

2.1. The difference

★ Method

We study the sign of the difference $a_{n+1}-a_n$

  1. We calculate $a_{n+1}-a_n$ and bring it to a simple form (common denominator, common factor).
  2. If $a_{n+1}-a_n>0$ for every $n$, the sequence is strictly increasing.
  3. If $a_{n+1}-a_n<0$ for every $n$, the sequence is strictly decreasing.

Example 1

$a_n=\frac{n+2}{2n+1}$.

$$a_{n+1}-a_n=\frac{n+3}{2n+3}-\frac{n+2}{2n+1}=\frac{(n+3)(2n+1)-(n+2)(2n+3)}{(2n+1)(2n+3)}=\frac{-3}{(2n+1)(2n+3)}<0.$$

The sequence is strictly decreasing.

2.2. The ratio

★ Method

We compare the ratio $\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}$ with $1$

  1. First we check that all the terms are strictly positive. Otherwise the method does not apply.
  2. We calculate $\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}$ and simplify it.
  3. If the ratio is greater than $1$ for every $n$, the sequence is strictly increasing; if it is less than $1$, the sequence is strictly decreasing.

Example 2

$a_n=\frac{3^n}{n+1}$, with all terms strictly positive.

$$\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\frac{3^{n+1}}{n+2}\cdot\frac{n+1}{3^n}=\frac{3n+3}{n+2}>1,\ \text{because}\ 3n+3>n+2.$$

The sequence is strictly increasing.

2.3. Induction

★ Method

For a sequence given by $x_1$ and $x_{n+1}=g(x_n)$

  1. We check that $x_1<x_2$ (or $x_1>x_2$).
  2. We show that $x_k<x_{k+1}$ implies $x_{k+1}<x_{k+2}$ (or the reverse inequalities).
  3. Sometimes we first prove by induction that the terms lie in an interval, then study the sign of the difference $x_{n+1}-x_n$.

Example 3

$x_1=2$ and $x_{n+1}=\frac{x_n+3}{2}$. We show that $x_n<x_{n+1}$ for every $n\ge 1$.

  • Check: $x_2=\frac{5}{2}>2=x_1$.
  • Induction step: $x_k<x_{k+1}$ gives $x_k+3<x_{k+1}+3$, so $\frac{x_k+3}{2}<\frac{x_{k+1}+3}{2}$, that is, $x_{k+1}<x_{k+2}$.

By the principle of mathematical induction, the sequence is strictly increasing.

3. How do I choose the method?

Remember

  • The general term is a sum, or a fraction with $n$ in the numerator and the denominator → the difference.
  • The general term contains powers $q^n$ or long products, and the terms are positive → the ratio (it simplifies a lot).
  • I only have the relation $x_{n+1}=g(x_n)$ → induction.

Exercises

A. The difference method

  1. Study the monotonicity of the sequences:

    a) $a_n=3n-7$;

    b) $b_n=4-n^2$;

    c) $c_n=\frac{3n-1}{n+1}$.

  2. The difference and its sign.

    a) Show that the sequence $a_n=\frac{5-2n}{n+3}$, $n\ge 1$, is strictly decreasing.

    b) Let $b_n=n^2-6n+10$, $n\ge 1$. Calculate $b_1$, $b_2$, $b_3$, $b_4$ and $b_{n+1}-b_n$. Is the sequence monotonic? From which index on is it strictly increasing?

  3. Study the monotonicity of the sequences:

    a) $a_n=1+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}+\ldots+\frac{1}{n}$;

    b) $b_n=\frac{1}{n+1}+\frac{1}{n+2}+\ldots+\frac{1}{2n}$.

    Hint: for b) write $b_{n+1}$ carefully: which terms are added and which term disappears?

  4. Let $a$ be a real number and the sequence $a_n=n^2+an$, $n\ge 1$.

    a) Find the values of $a$ for which the sequence is strictly increasing.

    b) For $a=-3$, is the sequence increasing? Is it strictly increasing?

B. The ratio method

  1. Study the monotonicity of the sequences:

    a) $a_n=\frac{n+1}{4^n}$;

    b) $b_n=\frac{3^n}{2n+1}$.

  2. Study the monotonicity of the sequences:

    a) $a_n=\frac{1\cdot 4\cdot 7\cdot\ldots\cdot(3n-2)}{3\cdot 6\cdot 9\cdot\ldots\cdot(3n)}$;

    b) $b_n=\frac{(n+1)(n+2)\cdot\ldots\cdot(2n)}{4^n}$.

  3. Consider the sequence $a_n=n\cdot\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^n$, $n\ge 1$.

    a) Show that $\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\frac{2(n+1)}{3n}$.

    b) Compare this ratio with $1$, depending on $n$.

    c) Is the sequence monotonic? From which index on is it strictly decreasing?

  4. Consider the sequence $a_n=-\frac{2^n}{n}$, $n\ge 1$. A student writes: "$\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\frac{2n}{n+1}\ge 1$, so the sequence is increasing."

    a) Calculate $a_1$, $a_2$, $a_3$. Is the student's conclusion correct?

    b) Where does the student go wrong? Find the correct monotonicity of the sequence.

C. The induction method

  1. Study the monotonicity of the sequences given by recurrence, for $n\ge 1$:

    a) $x_1=1$, $x_{n+1}=\frac{x_n+8}{3}$;

    b) $y_1=10$, $y_{n+1}=\frac{y_n+8}{3}$;

    c) $z_1=4$, $z_{n+1}=\frac{z_n+8}{3}$.

    What do you notice?

  2. The sequence $(x_n)_{n\ge 1}$ has $x_1=9$ and $x_{n+1}=\sqrt{x_n+20}$, for $n\ge 1$.

    a) Prove by induction that $x_n>5$ for every $n\ge 1$.

    b) Prove by induction that the sequence is strictly decreasing.

  3. The sequence $(x_n)_{n\ge 1}$ has $x_1=1$ and $x_{n+1}=\frac{x_n}{1+x_n}$, for $n\ge 1$.

    a) Prove by induction that $x_n>0$ for every $n\ge 1$.

    b) Show that $x_{n+1}-x_n=-\frac{x_n^2}{1+x_n}$ and deduce the monotonicity of the sequence.

    c) Calculate $x_2$, $x_3$, $x_4$, guess the formula of the general term and prove it by induction.

  4. The sequence $(x_n)_{n\ge 1}$ has $x_1=3$ and $x_{n+1}=3-\frac{2}{x_n}$, for $n\ge 1$.

    a) Prove by induction that $x_n>2$ for every $n\ge 1$.

    b) Show that $x_{n+1}-x_n=-\frac{(x_n-1)(x_n-2)}{x_n}$ and deduce that the sequence is strictly decreasing.

D. Choose the method

  1. Study the monotonicity of the sequences:

    a) $a_n=\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt{n}$;

    b) $b_n=n+\frac{1}{n}$;

    c) $c_n=\frac{n^2}{n^2+1}$.

    Hint: for a) multiply by the conjugate.

  2. Decide which of the following sequences are monotonic. Justify your answer.

    a) $a_n=\frac{(-1)^n}{n}$;

    b) $b_n=n+(-1)^n$;

    c) $c_n=2n+(-1)^n$.

For those who want more

  1. Let $a$ be a real number and the sequence $x_1=a$, $x_{n+1}=x_n^2-2x_n+2$, for $n\ge 1$.

    a) Show that $x_{n+1}-1=(x_n-1)^2$ and $x_{n+1}-x_n=(x_n-1)(x_n-2)$.

    b) If $a\in(1;\ 2)$, show that the sequence is strictly decreasing.

    c) If $a>2$, show that the sequence is strictly increasing.

    d) For which values of $a$ is the sequence constant?

Frequently asked questions

When can I use the ratio to study the monotonicity of a sequence?

Only when all the terms of the sequence are strictly positive. Then, if aₙ₊₁ / aₙ > 1 for every n, the sequence is strictly increasing, and if the ratio is less than 1, it is strictly decreasing. For negative terms the conclusion is reversed, so it is safer to use the difference.

How do I show that a sequence is not monotonic?

You find three consecutive terms that go up and then down, or down and then up, for example a₁ < a₂ and a₂ > a₃. A single such counterexample is enough: the sequence can be neither increasing nor decreasing.

Why do we use induction for sequences given by a recurrence?

When we only know x₁ and the relation xₙ₊₁ = g(xₙ), we have no formula for xₙ, so we cannot compute the difference directly. We check the inequality for the first two terms and show that it passes from one step to the next, usually because the function g is increasing.
